# Portfolio design system

Two layers: **primitives** (raw color scales, no meaning attached) and **tokens** (semantic names that point at a primitive step and carry a job to do). Never wire a component straight to a primitive hex — always go through a token, so a future re-theme only touches one file.

Every number below is computed, not eyeballed: hex → linear sRGB → OKLab → OKLCH using Björn Ottosson's standard matrices, with contrast checked against the real WCAG 2.2 relative-luminance formula. Where a ratio is quoted, it's exact.

Companion file: `design-tokens.json` — same data, structured for fetch-and-use.

---

## 1. How the primitive scales were built

Each family gets 11 steps (50 → 950). One step in each brand family is fixed exactly to the hex you gave me (the "anchor"); the other ten are generated by holding hue constant and sliding lightness along a standard curve, with chroma scaled proportionally and clipped back into the sRGB gamut wherever it would go out of range. Semantic families (green, red, amber, blue, violet) don't have a given hex, so I picked hues and solved for a lightness that clears 4.5:1 against both white and the cream canvas, then built the rest of the scale around that.

Lightness target curve (OKLCH L):

| Step | 50 | 100 | 200 | 300 | 400 | 500 | 600 | 700 | 800 | 900 | 950 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L | .980 | .955 | .910 | .860 | .795 | .720 | .635 | .545 | .435 | .320 | .235 |

Where each brand anchor landed:

| Family | Given hex | Landed at | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Background (cream) | `#FCF8EC` | 50 | Near-white, L = 0.979 |
| Primary (gold) | `#FDD35A` | 300 | L = 0.881, closest to the 300 target |
| Secondary (ink) | `#1E1E1E` | 950 | L = 0.235 — almost exactly the 950 target, and it's perfectly achromatic (C = 0.000), so this whole family is a true gray, not a tinted one |
| Accent 1 (orange) | `#FF702E` | 500 | L = 0.711 |
| Accent 2 (lavender) | `#D3D2FA` | 300 | L = 0.877 |

All five semantic anchors (green, red, amber, blue, violet) independently landed closest to step **700** once solved for accessibility — which turned out to be a useful coincidence: it means "700" is the one consistent step across every semantic family that's safe to use as text-on-cream or as a white-text-safe fill.

Two families are worth calling out specifically:

- **`neutral`** is not tied to any brand hex. It's an independent UI gray with a faint warm undertone (hue ≈ 91.5°, the same direction as cream) so it doesn't clash with the rest of the palette. It's separate from `ink` on purpose — see §5.
- **`violet`** shares its hue (286°) with `lavender` (Accent 2) but is a distinct scale. Lavender's anchor sits at step 300 — a pastel, meant for backgrounds. Text at that lightness would be unreadable, so `violet` re-anchors the same hue at step 700, dark enough to work as link text. Same family of purple, two different jobs.

## 3. Contrast rules this system enforces

Two WCAG 2.2 clauses matter here, and they're different requirements:

- **§1.4.3 (text contrast).** Normal text needs 4.5:1 against its background; large text (≥24px regular or ≥18.66px bold) needs 3:1. AAA is 7:1 / 4.5:1.
- **§1.4.11 (non-text contrast).** Anything that identifies a UI component or its state — an input's border, a button's edge, a selected-tab indicator — needs 3:1 against whatever's next to it, unless the shape/icon already carries that information without color.

Both get checked, separately, for every token below. A color can pass one and fail the other — that's not a bug, it's why there are two rules.

Genuinely useful things this caught:

- **`gold.300` (the literal Primary hex) sits at only 1.35:1 against the cream canvas.** Light yellow next to a warm off-white is close in luminance even though the hues look distinct to the eye. Every Primary button therefore carries a mandatory dark border — see §5.
- **White text fails on the literal Accent-1 hex** (`orange.500`, 2.76:1). Dark ink text works there instead (6.04:1), or step down to `orange.700` if white is non-negotiable.
- **All five semantic families land their AA-safe anchor at the same step (700)**, which is why every chip, badge, button, and status-text token below reads consistently across success/error/alert/info/link.

## 5. Brand rules — do and don't

**Primary (gold) is a fill color, not a text color.**
`gold.300` (the exact brand hex) reads at only 1.35:1 against the cream canvas — both colors are light, so despite looking very different, they're close in luminance. Don't put it behind text or rely on it alone to mark a component boundary. Do pair any gold fill with dark (`ink.950`) foreground content, and give Primary buttons a mandatory 1.5–2px dark border so the shape reads even for someone with low vision.

**Accent-1 (orange) needs a text-color swap depending on what's under it.**
`orange.500` (the exact brand hex) works with `ink.950` text (6.04:1) but fails with white (2.76:1). If a component needs white label text — most CTA buttons do — use `orange.700` as the fill instead of the raw brand hex. That's why the Accent button token above starts at 700, not 500. Reserve `orange.500` itself for large decorative blocks and icons paired with dark text.

**Accent-2 (lavender) is a light-on-dark color, not a light-on-light one.**
At L = 0.877, `lavender.300` (the exact brand hex) is close to white — fine as a background tint on the light canvas, unusable as foreground text or icon there. It earns its keep as the inverse-link and inverse-accent color on dark (`ink.950`) surfaces, where the same hex hits 11.41:1.

**Alert (amber) sits close in hue to Accent-1 (orange) and Primary (gold).**
27° separates amber from orange, 32° separates it from gold — tight enough that hue alone won't reliably tell them apart, especially for anyone with a color-vision deficiency. Never let a warning rely on color alone: pair `chip.alert` / `text.alert` / `icon.alert` with a label or a distinct icon shape (WCAG §1.4.1, "use of color," applies regardless of contrast ratio).

**`ink` and `neutral` are two different grays on purpose.**
`ink` is the exact Secondary brand color — perfectly achromatic, reserved for the Secondary brand moments (dark hero/footer sections, the Secondary button, `text.primary`). `neutral` is a separate, independently-built gray with a faint warm undertone, used for everything generic — borders, field backgrounds, disabled states, dividers. Don't use `ink` steps where a `neutral` token is called for; the two scales aren't numerically identical even though they look similar in a swatch.

**Placeholder text is not a label.**
`field.text.placeholder` sits at 3.22:1 against its own field background — under the 4.5:1 required for real text, because WCAG doesn't classify placeholder copy as text that needs to meet that bar. That's the exemption talking, not a green light to skip a real `<label>`. Every field needs a persistent visible label at `field.text.label` or darker; the placeholder is a hint, never the only name for the field.

**Disabled tokens are exempt from contrast minimums — use that exemption narrowly.**
`text.disabled`, `icon.disabled`, and every `*.disabled` fill in the button/chip/field tables intentionally fall below 4.5:1 (some below 3:1). That's allowed under §1.4.3's disabled-content carve-out, and it's also a signal to the eye that the thing genuinely can't be interacted with. Don't reuse a disabled token for anything a person is actually meant to read or act on.

**Every focus state uses `gold.700`.**
Buttons, links, fields, tabs — one consistent focus color across the whole system (`border.focus` / `link.focus` / field focus border). It clears 3:1 against both the cream canvas (4.70:1) and the dark ink surface (3.34:1), so it works as a focus ring regardless of what's behind it.

## 6. Dark ("inverse") sections

The palette supports dark bands (nav, hero, footer, tooltips) as a first-class pattern, not an afterthought — `ink.950` is a real brand color, not a synthetic dark-mode invention.

On an `ink.950` background: primary text is `cream.50` (15.70:1), secondary text is `neutral.300` (10.92:1), links default to `lavender.300` (11.41:1, and yes — that's the literal Accent-2 hex doing double duty), and interactive borders need `neutral.700` or darker to clear 3:1. Decorative dividers on dark surfaces can go as subtle as `neutral.800` (2.10:1) since they're not identifying anything.

## 7. Using the JSON

`design-tokens.json` mirrors this document exactly — same hexes, same OKLCH, same contrast numbers, computed from the same script so the two files can't drift apart.

- `primitives.<family>.scale.<step>` — raw color, `{hex, oklch, isBrandAnchor}`
- `tokens.<category>.<...>` — semantic token, `{value, ref, oklch, usage, contrast?, notes?}`

Fetch the tokens object and wire it straight into components; treat `primitives` as the layer only design tooling (Figma variables, Tailwind config, Style Dictionary) should touch directly.
