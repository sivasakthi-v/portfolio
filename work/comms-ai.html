The Brief

Where AI and communications expertise become one.

Comms.AI is a professional-grade platform for PR practitioners, in-house communications teams, and startup founders managing their own narrative. It combines structured guidance with AI generation across every major communications deliverable — from press releases and key messages to social strategy, narrative frameworks, and crisis communications.

The design challenge was significant: AI tools often feel like magic boxes. Everything in, or nothing out. Comms.AI was built to be the opposite — a transparent, structured, expert-guided process where AI is a collaborator, not a replacement for thinking.

The Problem Space

PR expertise is expensive. Poor communications is more so.

Most growing companies can't afford a senior PR consultant for every piece of communications they produce. The result is inconsistent messaging, missed narratives, and press releases that don't land. Comms.AI gives any team access to a structured, expert PR process — without needing to hire one.

Design Decisions

The thinking behind the interface.

01

Structure before generation

The most important decision was sequencing. Users don't open the app and start typing — they first answer a structured questionnaire, which extracts the raw material for AI generation. This produces dramatically better output than free-form prompting because the AI works from organised information, not vague intent. The questionnaire is the product's core innovation, not the AI itself.

02

Dynamic Intelligence as a named, visible feature

AI regeneration is given a name — "Dynamic Intelligence" — and a consistent visual state. Rather than hiding the AI behind a generic "Regenerate" button, the system makes intelligence visible: users see what's being regenerated, how many regenerations remain, and the quality of the result. This transforms AI from a black box into a collaborator with a clear interface.

03

The four states of Dynamic Intelligence

Dynamic Intelligence has four distinct states: Default (content generated, Regenerate available), Updated (new version, comparing to previous), Regenerating (animated loading), and Limit Reached (credits exhausted, buy-more CTA). Each is visually distinct and communicates status without ambiguity — preventing the anxiety of not knowing whether the AI is working, stuck, or done.

04

Content view flexibility: List, Table, Card, Calendar

Different professionals think about their work differently. A PR manager tracking deadlines thinks in calendar view; a content writer comparing articles thinks in card view. The content view toggle respects this diversity without forcing a single mental model — and it's persistent across sessions, so the system learns how each user prefers to work.

05

A dark theme designed for long sessions

Comms.AI users spend significant time drafting, revising, reviewing. The dark navy theme reduces eye strain during extended sessions while creating a visual seriousness that matches professional PR work. Hot-pink and purple gradient accents provide contrast for interactive elements without visual noise — every colour choice tested for accessibility contrast ratios.

Dynamic Intelligence

Four states. One experience of AI.

The cornerstone of Comms.AI's AI layer — each state communicates clearly what the system is doing, what just happened, and what the user can do next.

State 01 · Default

Ready

Generated content with regenerations available — a visible "Regenerate · 3" control so users always know their remaining budget.

State 02 · Updated

New version ready

A new version generated and ready to accept or compare against the previous one — no destructive overwrites.

State 03 · Regenerating

AI at work

An active generation state with animated loading that communicates the AI is working — never a frozen, ambiguous screen.

State 04 · Limit Reached

Credits exhausted

A clear path to resolution rather than a dead end: "Consider buying credits to polish your PR for maximum impact."

Outcomes & Learnings

What Comms.AI taught about AI product design.

🧠 Questionnaires are better UX than prompts

Free-form prompting produces inconsistent results because users don't know what the AI needs. Structured questionnaires encode expert knowledge into the interface, extracting exactly the right inputs for high-quality generation. The questionnaire design is where most of the product thinking lives.

⚡ AI states need to be designed, not assumed

The four Dynamic Intelligence states weren't obvious at the start. They emerged from mapping every possible system condition and asking: what does the user need to know right now? Designing AI states with the same rigour as user flows is a practice every AI product needs.

💳 Credit models need empathy-first design

The "Credits Exhausted" state could easily feel punitive. The approach was to make resolution as clear and frictionless as the limit itself — supportive language, not punishing. Monetisation moments are still UX moments.

🔄 Modularity at product scale is an investment

Eight modules under one design system required significant upfront investment in component architecture. But once the shared header, footer, content-view toggle, and creation-stage components were built, each new module added velocity rather than debt. At scale, modular design pays compound interest.
