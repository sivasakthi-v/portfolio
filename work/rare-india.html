Overview

One of my very first solo projects, and one of the most layered.

This was an early milestone. My first project as a solo UI/UX Designer at my company, leading design end-to-end across research, visual systems, interaction design, and motion prototyping. One project. Three complete design directions. An entire platform rethought.

The platform operates as a curated travel network, a bridge between conscious travelers and owner-run boutique hospitality experiences. Not a booking aggregator. A community built on storytelling, curation, and responsible-travel philosophy.

The Problem

Great brand. Weak digital experience. A gap worth designing into.

The brief was layered: make the website more interactive and experiential, put responsible tourism and brand identity at the heart of everything, and rethink how 60+ boutique hotels are discovered and booked.

The deeper problem wasn't aesthetics. It was about the kind of discovery experience this platform needed to offer. Most travel platforms are built for convenience-first UX, filters, price sorting, speed to book. This platform sits in a completely different space.

Here, the question isn't "where should I stay?", it's "what kind of journey do I want to experience?" That distinction changes every design decision.

"This is a story-first platform, not a conversion-first product. And that changes everything in UX decisions."

Old framing
"Where should I stay?", a logistics question.
The reframe
"What kind of journey do I want?", a meaning question.

Chapter 01 · First Pitch Direction

The Cinematic version, experimental & immersive.

The first pitch direction. Highly interactive, rich in motion and cultural detail. Built to explore: what would this platform feel like at its most immersive?

01 · Landing Page & Hotel Introduction

Cinematic transitions that lead you into each hotel's story.

Auto-driven transitions with gentle fades create a seamless flow between curated and dynamic hotel highlights. Full-width visuals preserve the richness of each property, every frame feels immersive and uninterrupted.

Motion is kept minimal and intentional, a calm, cinematic rhythm rather than distraction, gently leading users into each hotel's story.

Auto-transitionsFull-width immersionCalm motion rhythm
02 · Hotel Categories

The Chaitya arch, cultural grounding as a design system.

Each theme is framed within a Chaitya-inspired arch, a subtle nod to Indian architectural heritage, making the cultural grounding structural rather than decorative. Bold ornamental strokes and ambient color shifts distinguish categories without noise.

The central category stays in focus, slightly larger, more prominent, while surrounding options sit softly in the periphery. Users move through categories as a curated sequence, not a list.

Chaitya arch motifCultural hierarchyHorizontal guided flow
03 · Stays & Activities

Layered storytelling, visuals and narrative in balance.

Vertical scroll reveals stays and experiences in a gradual, immersive sequence. The arch acts as a framing device for each visual, supported by a bold ornamental stroke that maintains continuity across the section.

Contextual narratives appear alongside images, offering insight without interrupting the flow. A balanced interplay between visuals and text, where motion remains subtle and the experience feels fluid and focused.

Vertical scroll revealContextual narrativesVisual continuity
04 · Rare Spotlight

The ambient environment, each hotel as a moment, not a listing.

An immersive, guided flow between handpicked and dynamically selected stays. As users navigate through featured hotels, the surrounding environment adapts, subtly reflecting the tone and mood of each destination.

A responsive ambient backdrop shifts with each selection, allowing the mood of every space to carry through. Focused, immersive browsing where each hotel feels like a moment rather than a listing.

Ambient backdropMood-responsiveCurated selection flow
05 · Enquire Now

A gentle shift from exploration to action, no hard sell.

An animated banner that gently moves the experience from exploration to action. Travel visuals transition in the background at short, timed intervals, setting changing moods while maintaining continuity.

The enquiry message fades in first. Then a delayed call-to-action appears naturally, introduced, not forced. Motion uses pacing and layering to create anticipation while preserving the calm, cinematic tone.

Timed background transitionsDelayed CTA revealExploration → Action
06 · Responsible Tourism

A quiet thread, not a banner. Not a checkbox.

Placed consistently across the journey, dynamically, this element reinforces the brand's commitment to conscious travel. The visual stays soft and grounded, letting the message sit naturally within the experience rather than demanding attention.

Key principles appear as a continuous thread, connecting every page back to a deeper, more mindful way of exploring. Motion and interaction remain minimal, this is a reminder, not a feature.

↳ Visual Language

Design roots that trace back to culture, not just style.

The visual language of the Cinematic version draws from India's architectural and cultural heritage, the Chaitya arch as a recurring structural frame, ornamental strokes as accents, and a palette that balances richness with restraint.

The contemporary expression layer brings it together in execution, arch motif, typographic hierarchy, motion language, and color cohesion. Cultural roots meeting modern interaction.

Chapter 02 · Research-Led Direction

The Earthy version, research-led & brand-true.

Built from the ground up through research and real brand understanding. This direction received the highest satisfaction from internal testing and senior executives, for both brand feeling and usability.

01 · Booking Engine + The Letter

A booking flow that writes you a personal letter.

With my lead designer's vision on the overall flow, we built a 6-step booking process: dates & guests → preferred room → particular interests → in-house activities → food preferences → additional note.

Alongside the flow, a blank letter becomes visible, writing itself automatically as users make each selection. By the end, a full personal letter is ready, sent as booking confirmation to their email.

The intent: make users feel involved, give the platform a sincere and personal touch, and increase booking completion while reducing abandonment, by making booking feel like participation, not transaction.

Design decision

We could have designed a fast, frictionless booking flow. We chose the Letter instead, because these users aren't booking a commodity. A letter is the right artifact for a meaningful journey.

6-step structured flowLive letter generationReduces abandonment
02 · "Surprise Me"

For travelers who have a vibe, but not a destination.

A 5-step experiential filtering flow, for travelers who don't want to scroll through a grid, who have a vibe in mind but need the right place to surface. Each step asks one clear question: escape theme → landscape → stay experience & amenities → activities → food and season.

Upon selection, the system curates 3 results to choose from. Simple. Decisive. Emotionally resonant. It solves decision paralysis on curated platforms, and feels like a conversation, not a filter panel.

5-step emotional filtering3 curated resultsSolves decision paralysis
03 · Seasonal Collections

The road to your destination, one season at a time.

While discussing how to bring seasons into the platform, one keyword kept surfacing: "road / way to a destination." That became the visual concept.

Collections filtered by best season to visit, Winter, Summer, Spring, Monsoon. Each opens with a banner image of a focused road during that season, evoking what it would feel like to travel there right now. Not just a filter, an emotional entry point into a season's worth of stays.

A majority of travelers plan around seasons anyway. This makes that behavior feel natural and emotional rather than mechanical.

Seasonal filteringRoad visual conceptEmotional entry point
04 · Hotel List & Magic Filters

Level 1 filtering, one tap to the right landscape.

The hotel list page opens with what I call Magic Filters, a top-level filter layer using paired landscape and theme tags (with elegant icons) to cut through decision-making before the user reaches a full filter panel.

Want a beach view? A forest retreat? A heritage hotel? A mountain stay? One tap surfaces the right subset. No cognitive load. Underneath sits a classic side filter for location, price, activities, and collections, but the magic filters handle the first, most important moment of intent.

Magic Filters (Level 1)Landscape + theme tagsReduces decision friction
05 · Our Story · Ginkgo Biloba

A brand story told through the life cycle of a plant, on scroll.

Together with my lead designer and product manager, we brainstormed the idea of "life stages of a plant" as a metaphor for the brand's journey. I planned the layers and designed it using the brand symbol, the Ginkgo Biloba.

I experimented with Gen-AI image generation to visualize an approximately real Ginkgo Biloba life cycle, blending it with the story of the brand, in an animation that unfolds naturally as you scroll. A brand story that earns its reading rather than demanding it.

Key Decisions

The choices that defined the thinking behind the work.

Three decisions shaped the entire project, each with a clear reason, not just an instinct.

01

Three directions, not one iteration path

Instead of iterating on a single direction, we explored three fundamentally different visions: Cinematic, Earthy, and Minimal. Each tested a different set of values.

Why it mattered

The brief contained competing values, immersive storytelling and responsible clarity. Divergent directions let us test those against real designs, not assumptions.

02

Cultural grounding as structure, not decoration

The Chaitya arch in the Cinematic version wasn't aesthetic, it was structural, framing every feature section as a deliberate nod to Indian architectural heritage.

Why it mattered

This platform represents India's boutique hospitality. Generic modern UI would feel hollow. Rooting the language in cultural form gives it specificity that can't be borrowed.

03

The Booking Letter, emotion over efficiency

We could have designed a fast, frictionless flow. We chose the Letter, a live document that writes itself alongside booking, sent as personalised confirmation.

Why it mattered

These users aren't booking a commodity. A letter matches the brand's philosophy, and practically, it increases engagement and reduces abandonment.

Outcome

What changed, and what the work taught.

Earthy v2

Highest satisfaction from internal testing and senior executives

For both brand feeling and usability, showing that research-led design and emotional resonance don't trade off against each other.

Live

The Minimal version went live

Stakeholders settled on the Minimal direction, content-oriented, minimalist, brand-color focused. Their vision was clarity, and they didn't want to extend the brand identity beyond what exists. Good design also means serving the client's vision.

Booking

The Letter concept validated a broader principle

The booking + letter feature was the most discussed concept internally. Delightful, emotionally-considered UX has a functional business case: completion rate, abandonment reduction, brand differentiation.

System

Three complete design systems, not just screens

Each direction came with its own visual language, component library, motion principles, and interaction patterns, across a full responsive website and admin panel.

Reflection

What I'd do differently. What I'll carry forward.

Looking back, I wish I had pushed harder on usability testing earlier, particularly for the Cinematic version's experimental interactions. Some motion choices were made intuitively, and real user feedback would have helped validate or recalibrate them before they were fully built.

"The best decision I made was letting the three directions exist without trying to make them converge. The tension between them was where the real learning happened."

I also learned the difference between designing for a brand and designing for a product. A brand lives on feeling, narrative, and identity. A product lives on task completion and clarity. The Earthy version worked best because it held both, it understood the platform while staying true to the brand.

Keeping the brand under wraps actually shaped this write-up in a useful way. It pushed me to write about decisions and reasoning rather than leaning on brand recognition. A recruiter reading this should know: the thinking here is mine.
