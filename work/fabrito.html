TL;DR

The short version: Fabrito is one brand with three very different products for India's fabric trade, a mobile app where sellers digitise inventory, a web admin where the operations team curates supply and runs orders, and a B2B and B2C website where buyers discover fabric and order swatches, samples, and bulk. Executive Summary

Three interconnected products around one marketplace loop.

Fabrito connects Indian fabric sellers and mills with garment brands, boutiques, designers, and retail buyers. I designed the whole experience as a system of three products: a mobile app where sellers onboard, digitise, and manage inventory; a web admin console where the Fabrito operations team curates supply, orchestrates orders, and manages every actor on the platform; and a customer-facing website where buyers discover fabrics, order swatches and samples, and place bulk orders.

Across the three products I designed 900+ screens, popups, and component states, covering the full operational loop of a managed marketplace: seller KYC and approval, AI-assisted cataloguing, quality-controlled publishing, three order types with negotiation and fulfilment, payments and ledgers, collections and catalogue sharing, and a reverse marketplace for surplus fabric.

The Problem

India's fabric supply chain is rich, but deeply fragmented.

Small and mid-size mills hold huge inventories with no digital storefront. Cataloguing fabric is technical and slow, and discovery still happens over phone calls, WhatsApp photos, and physical swatch books.

Seller

Near-zero-effort cataloguing

Most sellers cannot fill a 20-field spec sheet per SKU. Digitising fabric had to feel like taking a photo, not doing data entry.

Operator

Control over trust

Unverified sellers, unreviewed listings, or wrong specs would destroy buyer trust. The operator needed a say before anything went live.

Buyer

Retail feel, B2B mechanics

Buyers wanted filters, variants, and collections with the reality of MOQs, swatches, sampling, pay terms, freight, and GST underneath.

The principle that shaped all three products

A managed marketplace. Everything a seller submits flows through an operations layer before it reaches a buyer, and every buyer order flows back through the same layer for negotiation and fulfilment.

Product 01 · Seller Admin (Web Console)

The operational heart of the marketplace.

The console the internal team uses to run Fabrito. A five-module left navigation, Dashboard, Inventory, Orders, Sellers, and Customers, plus a cross-cutting Requests centre, notifications, and role-based users.

01

Seller management & KYC

The full lifecycle with reversible states, All, Active, Approval Pending, Rejected, Blocked, Archived, with reason-capture on every destructive action and restore paths back to Active. Nothing is a dead end.

02

Inventory, QC & publishing

A moderated pipeline: Pending to Approve, Pending to Publish, Live, with controlled exits and restore throughout. A fabric physically cannot go live until its web details exist, and the error state teaches the rule.

03

Order management

Three order types, Sample, Swatch, and Bulk Sale, in one pipeline. Fabric-level statuses roll up into order-level status, with a Ship Fabrics flow that generates the Fabrito invoice.

04

Customer management

A lightweight CRM: revenue realised, active and pending orders, even fabrics sitting in a customer's cart, so ops gets a sales signal, not just a record.

05

Requests centre

Every inbound ask in one place, Edit, Help, and Account-Delete queues, each needing a mandatory "action taken" note before resolving, which quietly builds an internal knowledge base.

06

Fabric records, layered

Details and images, technical specifications, AI-generated marketable copy (editable, never blindly trusted), variant grouping, plus a full history and product-movement audit trail.

Product 02 · Seller App (Mobile)

Sellers are not catalogue managers.

The app translates the marketplace to the person standing in a fabric godown with a phone. Every flow is compressed to the minimum: photograph the fabric, let AI do the writing, tap through statuses.

Onboarding runs through a guided KYC, the exact documents the admin console verifies, then an approval-pending state. Before setup completes, the app runs in a restricted empty-state home with a persistent "setup profile" prompt. Sellers can look around, but the system protects the marketplace until configuration and approval are done.

Home

The business at a glance

A KPI dashboard: monthly earnings with trend, orders, receivables and payables, and a Low Stocks rail where quantity updates inline, because a stock-out on a live fabric hurts both seller and platform.

Add fabric

AI-assisted, batch-first

A seller adds several fabrics in one session, photographs each, and lets AI generate the name, description, and details before confirming the specs. Cancelling the batch warns, honestly, that all of it is deleted.

Ledger

Money, made legible

Total outstanding and estimated overdue, debit and credit entries tied to receipts and credit notes, and downloadable statements. Sellers see exactly where their money is.

Product 03 · Website (B2B & B2C)

Where the curated catalogue meets the buyer.

From a designer ordering swatches to a brand placing a bulk programme. The full retail surface, landing to gallery to product to cart to account, with B2B mechanics running throughout, and before-login and after-login variants of every major journey.

01

Landing & positioning

The offer splits into Ready Fabrics and Made-to-Order, each with its own "how to order" path, spotlight stories, and a WhatsApp-first contact, for an audience that often still buys offline.

02

Textile-literate discovery

A 16-facet filter system, type, structure, content, weight, width, count, MOQ, finish, colour, and more, plus product pages with 30+ variants, live totals including taxes and freight, and an enquiry form with reference-image upload.

03

Cart & checkout

Built for B2B realities: per-item quantity editing with MOQ validation, out-of-stock flagging, GST and freight in billing, and order statuses that mirror the admin pipeline exactly, so both sides see the same truth.

04

Collections & catalogue sharing

Beyond a wishlist, buyers group fabrics into named collections and download them as a catalogue PDF, effectively self-serve line sheets.

05

Sell Surplus, the reverse marketplace

A dedicated flow lets brands and mills list dead-stock, turning a costly industry problem into marketplace supply.

06

Account, auth & support

Email and phone login with OTP and error states, address management, help and FAQ, and WhatsApp integration at every friction point, matching how this industry actually communicates.

Design System & Craft

One brand, three contexts, one system.

The Fabrito red anchors all three products, applied to a clean white operational UI in admin, a KPI-dense mobile UI in the app, and a retail-warm UI on the website.

01

Component libraries per product

Fabric cards, header navs as full before and after-login sets, order status chips, filter and sort components, calendars, address blocks, upload patterns, and success snackbars, all built as reusable components with variants.

02

State completeness

Every list ships with empty, loading, filtered-empty, error, and success states. Every destructive action has a confirm, a reason, and an undo-adjacent restore path.

03

Real content, not lorem ipsum

Designs use realistic fabric data, compositions, GSM, prices, MOQs, so stakeholder reviews happened on truthful screens.

04

Design for operations

Reason capture, audit trails, and mirrored statuses across products were deliberate system-design decisions, not interface polish.

Role, Process & Collaboration

Sole designer across all three products.

I was responsible end to end: understanding the textile domain, mapping flows, wireframing, high-fidelity UI, component systems, popups and edge states, and developer-ready organisation of the file, module-labelled sections, popup galleries, and page-link flow maps for before and after login.

I worked with minimal guidance from a senior designer, used as a sounding board on key patterns, and ran iterative reviews with the product manager and client. Feedback landed in dated update sections, while earlier explorations stayed archived in-file for traceability. Decisions have receipts.

How I approached it

Domain immersion first

The 16-facet filter taxonomy and spec structures came from studying how fabric is actually specified and traded.

Systems, not screens

Reason capture, audit trails, and mirrored statuses across products were system-design calls.

Feedback with a paper trail

Manager and client rounds are visible as version sections, superseded flows preserved in-file.

Built for handoff

Labelled sections and flow maps made 900+ screens navigable for engineering.
