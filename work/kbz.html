The Brief

One ecosystem, many surfaces — held to one standard.

KBZ is one of Myanmar's largest financial institutions. The work spanned two worlds under a single design language: the consumer-facing KBZ Pay Market — an 8M+ user commerce platform — and the KBZ Bank core-banking MiniApp suite, where regulated banking workflows meet everyday mobile expectations.

The challenge across both: take dense, compliance-heavy processes — identity verification, approvals, account configuration — and make them feel as clear and quick as any modern app, without ever weakening the controls a bank depends on.

The Problem Space

Banking UX fails when compliance and clarity are treated as opposites.

Regulated flows tend to become forms on top of forms. But users abandon what they don't understand, and staff make errors in interfaces that hide state. The work treated compliance and usability as the same problem — designing controls that are visible, legible, and fast for both customers and the agents behind them.

Flagship · Video Banking

KYC that happens before the queue, not in it.

The flagship of the suite: a pre-submission KYC-via-video system that front-loads verification so that when a customer reaches an agent, the hard part is already done.

Traditional video KYC makes the customer and agent do everything live — upload, verify, wait, repeat — while a queue backs up behind them. The Video Banking flow inverts that. Customers complete document upload and a live selfie verification up front, so their case arrives at an agent already structured and ready to review.

On the agent side, a priority-aware queue and clear assignment flows mean the right case reaches the right person without manual triage. State is always visible — who is waiting, what stage they're at, what's blocking them.

Customer side

Document upload + live selfie

Guided capture with clear success and retry states, so verification succeeds on the first attempt more often.

System side

Priority-aware queue

Cases are ordered and routed by priority, not just arrival time — urgent verifications don't wait behind routine ones.

Agent side

Assignment flows

Clear ownership and hand-off, so no case is dropped and no customer is left in an ambiguous state.

Governance & Admin

The controls a bank runs on — designed to be seen, not hidden.

Behind the customer apps sit the admin platforms. Their defining pattern is maker-checker: nothing consequential ships on one person's action alone.

Pattern

Maker-checker approval workflows

Across Retail Banking and NPS admin panels, every change is proposed by one role and approved by another. The design makes the state of each request — drafted, pending, approved, rejected — a first-class, glanceable element, so accountability is never buried in a log.

Config

Account configuration management

Structured controls for retail banking account settings, built so operators can make precise changes with confidence — and so mistakes are caught before they reach production, not after.

Support

Agent-side CMS for chat

Quick replies, help articles, and internal notes give support agents a shared source of truth — turning consistent, correct answers into the path of least resistance.

Insight

Survey & NPS lifecycle

Full survey lifecycle management with NPS tracking — from authoring to publish to analysis — so the bank can listen to customers systematically, not sporadically.

Design Decisions

The thinking behind regulated-product UX.

01

Front-load verification, protect the queue

Moving KYC to a pre-submission step was the highest-leverage decision in Video Banking. It shortens the live agent interaction, reduces abandonment mid-call, and lets a priority-aware queue do triage that would otherwise fall on staff. The customer does the work once, calmly, before anyone is waiting on them.

02

Make maker-checker a visible state, not a hidden rule

Approval workflows often live in permissions and logs. Here, the request's status is a primary UI element on every admin surface. Turning governance into something you can see at a glance is what makes it usable — operators trust a system whose state they can read without hunting.

03

Design both sides of every interaction

Every customer MiniApp was designed alongside the agent or admin surface that operates it. A support chat is only as good as the agent CMS behind it; a KYC flow is only as fast as the queue that receives it. Designing the pair — not just the pretty half — is what makes the system actually run.

04

Gamified ranking to make performance legible

Leaderboard dashboards use gamified ranking indicators so branch and individual standing is readable instantly. The goal wasn't novelty — it was legibility: turning rows of numbers into a hierarchy a manager can act on in seconds.

Outcomes & Learnings

What designing at bank scale taught.

🔐 Compliance is a design material

Maker-checker, KYC, audit trails — these aren't obstacles to design around; they're the material you design with. The best regulated UX treats a control as something to make legible, not something to hide behind a modal.

🤝 The agent is a user too

Every customer-facing screen has an operator behind it. Designing the agent CMS, the queue, and the admin panel with the same care as the consumer app is what separates a demo from a system that a bank can actually run.

⏱️ Sequencing beats speed

Front-loading KYC didn't make any single screen faster — it re-sequenced the whole flow so the slow part happens when no one is waiting. Often the biggest UX win isn't a faster screen; it's a better order of operations.

🌏 Scale demands restraint

With millions of users and staff across many roles, every added option multiplies. Working across KBZ Pay and the bank made restraint a survival trait: fewer, clearer decisions travel better across a large, diverse audience.
